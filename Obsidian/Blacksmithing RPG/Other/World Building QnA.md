
#### **Is there a government? Where is the game based?**

The game is based around a small town and a large city and the wilderness in between and around them. The large walled town is called [[Koran]], and is ruled by a dukedom. It is part of a larger kingdom. The small town is named -NAME-HERE- and has a mayor that is chosen by the people. Around the wilderness are other smaller towns such as the elven village of [[Illenal]] ruled by a council of elders, the orc village of [[Urodh]], ruled by a tribal leader.

#### **What type of currency? Who provides the money? How do you get money?**

The [[Currency|currency]] is gold, silver, and bronze coins. One gold coin is worth 10 silver coins, 1 silver coin is worth 10 copper coins. 

You earn money through labor, crafting, or seeking treasures in the wild.

#### **What can money get you in this world?**

Money can secure you food, shelter, and comforts, or buy you passage to distant lands and exotic wares. Gold coins are valuable and used to buy higher end equipment and magical items. A silver coin can get you a night at the cheapest inn, it is a laborer’s work for half a day, or a mile on a ship (roughly). A copper coin can purchase you a candle, a sack, or a whetstone as an example. A loaf of bread would be roughly 3 bronze coins. 

#### **Who buys your work (as a blacksmith)?**

[[Adventurers]] buy weapons, common people buy tools and other objects. The dukedom also buys their gear from the best blacksmith in the city chosen by the blacksmithing guild. 

#### **How do you get the materials for smithing?**

The ways to get materials for smithing are as follows: Mining for ore, purchasing ore/ingots from the market, chopping wood, harvesting leather, obtaining magical artifacts from treasure.

#### **Are there caves? Do dungeons exist?**

There are caves to mine ore dotted around the wilderness. The biggest ones are already being mined by certain rich merchants. There are smaller or hidden and unknown caves that are ignored by richer merchants that the player can go in for free. The merchants often make you pay a fee to enter their mines.

There are numerous small dungeons left from civilization's past. The origins of these dungeons have been long since forgotten. Adventures from all over are still constantly exploring and clearing these dungeons, because if not done the monsters from these dungeons can overflow into the surface world. Along with this they are also explored for their seemingly endless supply of monstrous resources and treasure. 


#### **How do adventurers actually make a living?** 

To become an adventurer, one must first register at the adventurer’s guild. Once done you are allowed to partake in quests based on your rank. These quests can be anything from requests of help from people, frequent quests to tackle dungeons that require clearing before overflowing, or anything in between. 

Along with taking these quests that give rewards, adventurer’s can also sell different resources acquired from both killing monsters inside of these dungeons, and from treasures inside of the dungeon themselves. Many monsters drop very useful materials that are often sought out by different people. 


#### **Are there different species/Races? How do they all work together?**

There are 9 different races, the [[Humans]], the [[Dwarves]], the [[Elves]], the [[Goblins]], the [[Orcs]], the [[Dracari]], the [[Taurians]], the [[Nekojin]], and the [[Kitsunari]]. 

Humans are the most common race. 

The Dwarves have begun to intermingle with humans and live in the same towns, however in the past they lived separately in underground cities. These cities still exist, but more and more dwarves have been moving out from them. 

The Elves still live in secluded communities and villages. There are a few elves that live in human cities but these are very few and tend to be high level mages and warriors looking for adventure.

The Goblins are a freakish group that despise humans. They have low intelligence but are constantly trying to attack human villages. 

The Orcs are thought to be allied with the goblins, and this was true in the past, but within the last decade great changes have been undergone in the orcish leadership and mindset. Now they seek a peaceful life, but they are still despised and discriminated against by most species. 

The Dracari are dragons that can take on a human form. They are very rare, with only a few being alive in each region. Most think that they are simply myth, a legend of old, but the ancient among the living still remember them from the wars of the past. 

The Taurians are another rare race but the ones that are around live around humans. 

The Nekojin and Kitsunari are much the same in the way that they live in most human settlements. 


#### **Does magic exist? What kind of magic is it? Who can use magic? Is it hard to use?**

[[Mana]] flows through every living thing, but only some can use it directly. Along with it being a force inside of a person there is also a type of physical mana called “[[Flowing Mana]]”, which is a glowing white liquid. Flowing mana can be used to infuse objects with mana and “jumpstart” magical objects. Do bear in mind that if you are unable to cast magic directly, bathing in mana cannot unlock this ability. This practice and experiment has long since been banned by the continental mage association. Being able to use magic without a magical object is something you are born with, but a second form of casting magic has recently been discovered. This form of magic is called “[[Rune Magic|Runes]]”. Anyone can learn and engrave magic runes on an object. If it were then either imbued with mana by a mage able to cast magic, or bathed  in flowing mana, it could then be used to cast the spell that was engraved on it.

  

#### **Is it used in everyday life? If it is, is it expensive?**

Magic is commonly used in combat mainly by mages who can cast magic directly. Often other classes of heroes will have their weapons imbued with magic by high level mages. Runes can also be used to augment weapons, but most stay away from this due to it being considered a crude and weaker form of magic. 

Recently certain academic mages have been researching what they call magi-tech. Magi-tech is a form of magic imbued into mechanical objects. This is different from runes. Instead of etching an ancient language they instead place a rare type of crystal that was recently discovered in some dungeons into an object, and channel magic into it. Magi-tech is currently quite expensive as it requires these special and rare crystals that have been found in only a few dungeons. These Academic mages have high hopes that they will be able to perfect this magic and use it to augment, automate, and assist people in their everyday life. 

  

#### **What types of jobs are there that use magic?**

The most common job that uses magic are adventurers who use magic to fight or aid their party. These mages often focus on one type of magic and learn it to a high proficiency.

Academic mages are mages who focus on learning more about magic. Some research the history of magic, some research and create new spells, and others focus on creating things out of magic to benefit society. 

Runesmiths are the name given to the people who often cannot cast magic directly, and instead engrave runes from an ancient language to channel flowing mana into power. This ancient language was discovered by an academic mage named Artherus Sneeveling. He managed to decode this language and learned that it was an ancient form of magic that doesn’t require the user to use mana, but instead uses “flowing mana” to power runes carved into objects. Most mages consider it “crude” because the discovered runes are quite simple in effect. Sadly Artherus passed away before he could finish translating the language, and his work has long since been lost. The only remnant of it are the runes he published before his death and the instructions on how to use them.
